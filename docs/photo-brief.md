# The photographs the site is waiting for

Every card on the site currently draws a generated "granule field" — circles
laid out from a hash of the record's slug — instead of a photograph. It is a
deliberate placeholder rather than a broken image, and it is stable and on
brand, but it is a placeholder.

This is the list of what to shoot, and what each frame has to be, so nothing
comes back cropped through the middle.

## Why these are not stock photographs

Three of the four groups below are claims, not illustrations. A project page
says *we supplied this*, a product page states the grading and density of *this
grade*, and the plant pages show *this plant*. A stock photograph of somebody
else's pour, or of another producer's granules with different grading, is a
picture that says something untrue on a page whose whole value is that its
numbers can be relied on. It also costs the site the one thing a competitor
cannot copy: evidence that the works exists and the lorries leave it.

The uses and the article covers are the exception — those illustrate a concept
rather than assert a fact, and a licensed stock photograph is honest there.
That licence is a decision for whoever owns the site, which is why it is not
made here.

## What the sizes mean

The site generates its own 480 / 768 / 1024 / 1440 / 1920 px versions of
whatever is uploaded, so supply the largest sensible original and let it do the
work. Anything under the stated width will be upscaled and look soft on a
laptop screen. Upload limit is 12 MB; JPEG or WebP.

A phone from the last few years is entirely adequate for all of this. Even
light matters far more than the camera — shoot in shade or on an overcast day,
not in direct midday sun, which blows out the pale granules and loses the
texture that makes them recognisable.

## Products — 7 photographs, square subject, at least 1440 px wide

Cropped to **1:1** on the card and **4:3** on the product page, so keep the
granules centred and filling the frame rather than running off one edge.

One per grade, and they must be visibly different from each other, because the
difference between the grades *is* the product.

| Grade | What the frame should show |
|---|---|
| لیکا سازه‌ای ۴–۱۰ | Granules filling the frame, a hand or a coin in shot for scale |
| لیکا سازه‌ای ۱۰–۲۰ | The same treatment — the size difference must read against the one above |
| لیکا ریزدانه ۰–۳ | Close, almost sand-like; the texture is the subject |
| لیکا شیب‌بندی ۳–۱۰ | Loose pile, the way it arrives on site |
| لیکا ژئوتکنیک ۱۰–۳۰ | The coarsest, with clear scale |
| لیکا باغبانی ۴–۱۰ | Washed and clean, lighter in colour than the construction grades |
| لیکا بام سبز ۸–۱۶ | Wet or damp, as it sits in a drainage layer |

Shoot all seven the same way — same background, same distance, same light — so
the row of cards reads as one set and the eye compares the granules rather than
the styling.

## Uses — 7 photographs, square subject, at least 1440 px wide

Cropped to **1:1** on the card and **16:9** on the page, so the subject needs
room above and below it or the wide frame will cut through it.

بتن سبک سازه‌ای · شیب‌بندی بام و کف‌سازی · پرکننده ژئوتکنیکی · بام سبز ·
کشاورزی و هیدروپونیک · عایق‌کاری حرارتی · فیلتراسیون و تصفیه

Work in progress beats a finished surface: a pump placing lightweight concrete,
a screed being floated, a drainage layer going down before the substrate covers
it. Once the job is finished the material is invisible, and a photograph of a
finished floor says nothing about what is under it.

## Projects — 6 photographs, at least 1440 px wide

Cropped to **3:2** on the card and **16:9** on the page. These are the ones that
have to be the actual sites, with the volumes already stated on each page.

## Articles — 16:9, at least 1440 px wide

Nine posts, four of them new. These illustrate rather than claim, so a licensed
stock photograph is legitimate here.

## The site-wide pictures

These live under Panel → Site images rather than on a record.

| Slot | Shape | Supply |
|---|---|---|
| Hero — desktop | 3:2, fixed | 1536 × 1024 |
| Hero — mobile | 0.64–0.78, follows the copy | 1280 × 1920 portrait, subject centred |
| Showcase — desktop | 1.7–2.3, follows the window height | 2560 × 1140, subject in the middle band |
| Showcase — mobile | **4:5, fixed** | 1080 × 1350 |
| Quality laboratory | 3:2, fixed | 1536 × 1024 |
| Plant exterior, kiln, screening | 4:3, fixed | 1600 × 1200 |

Two of them have no fixed shape, and that is not an oversight: both are
full-bleed backgrounds whose height comes from the screen or from the length of
the copy over them. There is no single export size that fits every handset, so
the rule for those two is to keep the subject in the middle and accept that the
edges go. Everything else on this page crops to a number.

Both hero and showcase images sit under a dark gradient so white text stays
legible over them — 45 to 72 per cent on the hero, 35 to 70 on the showcase. A
photograph will therefore reach the page darker than it looks in the file;
choose a bright, low-contrast one rather than a busy dark one.

## Category images — a different thing entirely

The four product categories have an image field that **never appears on the
page**. It is the preview picture when a link to that category is shared on
social media or a messenger. Roughly 1.91:1, at least 1200 × 630 px. Text in
the image is fine here — it is competing for attention in a feed, not
illustrating a page.

## Uploading

Panel → the record → the picture field. Each field now states the ratio it
crops to and the minimum width; read it before choosing the photograph rather
than after seeing the result. Nothing else needs doing — the derivatives, the
`srcset` and the lazy loading are generated on upload.
